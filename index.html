01. Photovoltaic Systems

Solar energy works through the Photoelectric Effect. When photons (light particles) hit a solar panel, they strike a semiconductor—usually Silicon. This silicon is "doped" with impurities to create a P-N junction, which acts like a one-way hill for electrons.

The energy from the sun knocks electrons loose from their atoms. Because of the internal electric field, these electrons are forced to flow in one direction, creating Direct Current (DC). A device called an Inverter then converts this into Alternating Current (AC) for your home.

  • Doping Process: Phosphorus is added to the top layer (N-type) and Boron to the bottom (P-type) to create the flow.
  • Efficiency: High-end monocrystalline panels now reach over 22% efficiency.

02. Wind Turbine Aerodynamics

It’s a common mistake to think wind "pushes" turbine blades like a sail. In reality, they work on Aerodynamic Lift, exactly like an airplane wing. The air moves faster over the curved top of the blade, creating low pressure that "sucks" the blade forward.

This rotational energy is sent into a Gearbox that steps up the slow rotation of the blades (about 15 RPM) to over 1,500 RPM. This high-speed shaft spins a Generator where magnets and copper coils create a massive flow of electrons.

  • Yaw Drive: A computer system that rotates the entire top of the turbine to face the wind.
  • Blade Pitch: The ability to twist the blades to capture more wind or "feather" them to stop safely during a storm.

03. Fossil Fuel Destruction

Non-renewables like coal and oil are stored "ancient sunlight" in carbon form. Burning them releases millions of tons of CO2, which traps heat in our atmosphere like a greenhouse.

This results in Ocean Acidification. As the oceans absorb the CO2, the water becomes acidic, dissolving the shells of sea creatures and killing the coral reefs that provide 25% of all marine life's habitat.
